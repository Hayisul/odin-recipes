Polenta

Description

Polenta is a traditional Italian dish made from coarsely ground cornmeal. Its origins can be traced back to ancient Roman times, when similar porridges were prepared using grains such as spelt and millet. Following the introduction of maize from the Americas in the 16th century, cornmeal polenta gradually became a staple of northern Italian cuisine, particularly in Veneto, Lombardy and Friuli.

Having grown up in Veneto, polenta holds a special place in my heart. Its simplicity, warmth and comforting texture evoke a sense of nostalgia and remind me of the culinary traditions of my homeland. Despite its humble origins, polenta is an incredibly versatile dish that lends itself to both traditional and contemporary cooking.

In Veneto, polenta is traditionally paired with mushrooms, small lagoon shrimp, slow-cooked stockfish or local cured meats. However, its mild flavour and adaptable texture make it an excellent companion to dishes from other culinary traditions, such as Mexican-style black beans and salsa, Moroccan spiced vegetables, Indian chickpea curry or Brazilian meat stews. Polenta also lends itself beautifully to modern culinary interpretations, from creamy Parmesan and truffle polenta to grilled slices topped with caramelised vegetables, crispy polenta fries or nourishing bowls with roasted vegetables and tahini.

Ingredients

Servings: 4

Preparation time: 5 minutes

Cooking time: 40–50 minutes

Base Ingredients

  • 250 g medium or coarse-ground cornmeal (yellow or white)
  • 1 l water
  • 8-10 g salt

Optional Ingredients

  • 30 g butter
  • 50 g grated Parmesan
  • 100 g shredded melting cheese (such as Asiago or Gouda)
  • Vegetable or chicken stock, as an alternative to water
  • Black pepper
  • Fresh herbs

Steps

  1. Bring the water to a boil in a heavy-bottomed pot and add the salt.
  2. Gradually pour in the cornmeal while whisking continuously to prevent lumps from forming.
  3. Reduce the heat to low and allow the polenta to simmer gently.
  4. Cook for 40-50 minutes, stirring regularly with a wooden spoon to prevent sticking and burning.
  5. Adjust the consistency by adding a little hot water if the polenta becomes too thick.
  6. Once the cornmeal is tender and the polenta reaches a smooth, creamy consistency, remove it from the heat.
  7. Optionally, stir in butter, cheese or other ingredients of your choice.
  8. Serve immediately while warm and creamy, or pour onto a wooden board or into a shallow dish and allow it to cool and firm up before slicing, grilling or frying.
